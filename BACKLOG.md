- 2026-08-28: Joint API-request audit with the aura-android session, scoring the iOS side against AEMET's per-key 50 req/min budget. - 2026-08-26: Cache `/observacion/convencional/todas` on its own hourly clock (API-savings item). The national observation feed updates once per hour and every record carries its own measurement time (`fint`), but `performRefresh` (`Aura/AEMETService.swift`) re-fetched it whenever *any* location's snapshot crossed the 1-hour staleness gate, which keys on `WeatherSnapshot.updated` (wall-clock write time, not the reading's own time). Now the observation call is gated on the stored `fint`: it's skipped entirely while `now < fint + 90min` (the hourly cadence plus a 30-minute publish-lag margin, because AEMET publishes each reading 20-40 min after the hour, so a bare +60 would re-download the same `fint` and waste the call). A `force` or single-location (`onlyINE`) refresh always fetches; a missing anchor falls through and fetches; a future-dated `fint` is clamped (`min(last, now)`) so a bad timestamp can suppress the fetch for at most the 90-min window, never indefinitely. The anchor persists in the App Group as `SharedCache.lastObservationFint`, written after each successful fetch from the freshest record's `.timestamp`. **Scope is observations only** — the forecast (own `elaborado` cadence), UV (daily) and especially avisos (irregular, drives notifications) keep their current behaviour, so a warning is never stale-cached. When the fetch is skipped but a location's forecast still needs rebuilding, each location reuses its last good station reading rather than blanking the observed card: `WeatherSnapshot.make(...)` gained a `previousObserved:` parameter that carries the prior snapshot's observed fields (temp, station, distance, metrics, full reading) all-or-nothing per station, so a fresh reading never mixes with a stale one. With your example — a 10:00 reading is next due at 11:30, so a refresh at 10:35 makes zero calls to `/todas`. ### Radar plan: "radar for the displayed location"
