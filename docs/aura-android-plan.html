Aura for Android

Bringing my AEMET weather app to Android as its own native app — as identical to the iPhone version as it can be, phones only, no watch and no tablet.

Native Kotlin + Compose minSdk 26 no backend Spanish-only

The recommendation, in one breath

Build it as a native Kotlin + Jetpack Compose app, hand-porting AuraKit's logic into a Kotlin :core module. Ship home-screen widgets with Jetpack Glance. Target a wide, modern floor. And skip the Play Store at first — hand family a signed APK link, graduating to Play's internal-testing track once the circle widens.

01

The tech-stack call

Three options weighed. The winner is the one with the smallest conceptual gap from what I already know and the least framework risk — which matters a lot starting with limited Android experience.

Recommended

Native Kotlin + Compose

Kotlin is Android's Swift; Compose is Android's SwiftUI. Every 2026 tutorial, sample and tool assumes this pair, and Glance (Compose-based widgets) comes as a first-class sibling. Smallest mental gap, no bridge layer that can lag platform releases.

Rejected

Kotlin Multiplatform

AuraKit already exists and works in Swift. KMP only pays off writing shared logic once, going forward, for both — adopting it now means a third rewrite or two parallel logic layers. More infrastructure to learn, worse for this situation.

Rejected

Flutter

A third UI paradigm and toolchain on top of Swift and Kotlin. Its platform-channel bridge makes the small native things (Glance, permissions, WorkManager) more awkward. I'm building two native apps that share a design language, not one shared binary.

SwiftUI → Compose, as reassurance

Most of what I know transfers as vocabulary, not new ideas. The real learning curve isn't the UI — it's the surrounding platform (the Activity/lifecycle model, permissions, background execution, Gradle), which has no SwiftUI equivalent.

04

OS support & test device

minSdk 26 · targetSdk 36

AndroidX itself raised its floor to API 23 in 2025, so Compose already assumes ≥ Android 6. Going below 26 buys a shrinking, low-value tail of old hardware that's expensive to test. API 26 reaches an estimated ~96–98% of active devices.

targetSdk 36 from day one: Play requires new apps to target Android 16 by Aug 31, 2026. Start there and never think about the deadline.

Buy a used Pixel a-series

A 6a / 7a / 8a, findable well under $200 secondhand: stock Android (matches Google's docs & samples), gets OS versions first, no aggressive OEM battery-killer to fight. New option: Pixel 10a (~$499, 7 years of updates).

Add a Samsung A-series later, only to catch Samsung's aggressive background-kill behaviour before a relative reports "the widget's stuck." Don't buy both up front.

🖥️ Emulator vs real device

The bundled emulator covers almost everything — UI iteration, navigation, network, themes, densities, even simulated GPS. A physical device is essential for real Doze/battery-kill behaviour (the big one for a weather app), OEM skins, real permission prompts, widget placement gestures, and rehearsing "can my mother sideload this from a link" — fundamentally a real-human test.

05

Google Play & distribution — the crux

A Play Developer account is required for any Play track — one $25 one-time fee, unlimited apps forever, non-refundable. Personal account (needs a government ID) is the right pick for a hobby project; the organization account (needs a free D-U-N-S number, up to 30 days) is exempt from the catch below but overkill here.

⚠︎ The new-account hurdle to understand precisely

Any personal account created after Nov 13, 2023 must, before requesting production access, run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days. The clock starts only once the release is approved and all 12 have opted in — drop-outs reset. (It was eased from 20 → 12 on Dec 11, 2024.)

This does not block internal testing or direct sideload — only full public production. An old pre-2023 personal account, if I have one, is grandfathered out — worth checking the Console before assuming I must clear it.

.aab vs .apk

Since Aug 2021, Play requires the App Bundle (.aab) upload format — Play's servers slice it into a per-device .apk. This only bites on Play; sideload and Firebase take a plain signed .apk directly.

App signing

Use Play App Signing: I hold an upload key, Google custodies the real signing key — losing my upload key is recoverable now (it wasn't in the old model). For anything outside Play I sign with my own key and must never lose it.

🕒 Recommended path

Start with (a) direct APK for the first one or two testers while it's rough — zero setup beyond a signed build. Once a wider family circle wants it day-to-day, move to (c) Play internal testing: the $25 buys the familiar "open the Play Store, tap install" flow without ever touching the production gate. Only chase full production if the app should be genuinely public — unlikely for a Spain-specific personal project.

06

The AEMET API-key wall

On iOS I'm the only user, so pasting my own free key once is a non-issue. The moment relatives run independent installs, that free-but-fiddly government-portal signup becomes a real onboarding wall that loses non-technical people before the app ever works.

Each user, own key

Matches iOS exactly, no shared-quota risk. But it works directly against "easy for family" — the whole wall, per person.

Recommended · this phase

One shared key I own

Baked in obfuscated (never plaintext, never committed, never printed). Zero friction — install and it just works. I'm the single point of failure if AEMET rate-limits, but the audience is a handful of households and the per-refresh call volume is modest.

A tiny proxy

Real key stays server-side, central control to rate-limit or swap. But it reintroduces a backend into an app whose whole philosophy is "no backend, the device is the hub." Revisit only if usage really grows.

07

Risks & gotchas

MITECO's incomplete cert chain

The server doesn't send a complete chain (iOS needed curl -k). Fix with a narrowly-scoped Network Security Config adding the missing anchor for that one host — not a global "trust everything." Confirm the exact missing intermediate first.

Background refresh & Doze

Android kills background work harder than iOS, and OEMs (Samsung especially) pile on their own battery-killers. WorkManager is the right tool, but the 3-hour widget refresh needs real-device testing for silent stalls after idle — the most likely "it's stuck" report.

Spanish NLG parity + locale

Beyond the seed gotcha, force es-ES formatting explicitly (decimal commas, dates) rather than trusting device locale — a relative's phone may be set to another language entirely. The app is Spanish-only regardless.

Location permission

Ask coarse, foreground-only (ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION) — kilometre accuracy is all the nearest-city logic needs. Never request background location: it triggers a separate, scrutinised Play review Aura doesn't need.

Relative effort, not a schedule — small medium large.

Setup

small

Android Studio, Compose empty-activity project at minSdk 26/targetSdk 36, Hello-World on emulator + physical device, :core module skeleton.

large — the bulk

Models → kotlinx.serialization; AEMETClient → Retrofit; SolarTimes (lowest risk); ForecastPhrase (seed parity); AQI/UV/wind + MITECO client (cert fix); CAP + RSS parsing. Unit tests against the existing fixtures.

3

large

Compose equivalents of the Aura*Card suite over a ported AuraSky. Where the SwiftUI→Compose mapping does the most work — expect this to feel the most familiar.

4

Widgets

medium

Glance home-screen widget(s), DataStore as the shared read path, WorkManager periodic refresh — validated against Doze on the real device. Most likely to surface Android-specific surprises.

5

Distribution

small effort · waiting cost

Register the account ($25), set up signing, ship a signed APK to myself + a tester, then stand up Play internal testing for the wider circle. Budget calendar time for opt-in flows.

6

Polish

ongoing

Icon/art parity, locale edge cases, OEM battery-optimization guidance for testers, and closing the feel-gap once real family feedback lands.

Quick FAQ

Do I need a developer account?

Yes, for any Google Play distribution — even the lightest testing track. Not required at all for direct APK sideload or Firebase App Distribution.

Does it cost money? How much?

$25, one time, for the Play Developer account — covers unlimited apps forever, non-refundable. Firebase App Distribution and direct APK sideload are both free.

What's the catch for a brand-new personal account?

Before publishing to full production, Play requires a closed test with 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days, and the clock only starts once the release is approved and all 12 have opted in. It does not block internal testing or direct sideload. Organization accounts and pre-Nov-2023 personal accounts are exempt.

What device should I buy?

A used or refurbished Pixel a-series (6a/7a/8a) as the primary — stock Android, cheap, closest to what Google's tooling assumes. Add a Samsung A-series later only to catch aggressive battery-optimization behaviour before a relative hits it.

How much OS-version support?

minSdk 26 (Android 8.0, 2017), reaching an estimated ~96–98% of active devices in 2026, without compatibility baggage for anything older than Compose's own dependencies already assume.

How do family and friends actually install it?

Easiest first: a direct signed APK link (one install-permission tap, no account on their end) while the app is rough. Once it's ready for a wider circle, move to Play internal testing so they install it the normal Play-Store way, with no "unknown sources" prompt.

What about the AEMET key every user needs?

Ship one shared key I own for this phase rather than making each relative register their own. Revisit toward per-user keys or a proxy only if usage grows meaningfully past a handful of households.
